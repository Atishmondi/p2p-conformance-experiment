# Conformance Checking in Procure-to-Pay: A Controlled ERP Experiment Distinguishing Temporal Variation from Control Failure

## Abstract

Conformance research offers mature detection but rarely isolates causes: ground truth in real logs is unknown, and delay is confounded with control failure. We ask how mining can distinguish tolerable temporal variation from undesirable control failures in procure-to-pay (P2P), and whether targeted ERP configuration can eliminate the latter without sacrificing flexibility. We run a controlled stochastic experiment around a six-step normative model (request–approval–order–receipt–invoice–payment) with five executable rules (R1–R5). Round 1 generates 120 cases (740 events; seed 42) across six scenarios (S1–S6) separating baselines, lognormal-wait delays under a business-hours clock, and injected deviations under fixed master data; Round 2 re-executes 60 cases (400 events; seed 7) after redesign. We triangulate PM4Py discovery (fitness, precision, alignments), a frozen rule checker, and statistics (chi-square, Fisher exact, Wilson intervals, Cohen h; Welch t for throughput). All 60 targeted deviations are detected with zero S1 false positives; delays surface only in performance analysis. After billing blocks, zero-tolerance matching, and re-approval gates, violations fall 60/60 to 0/60 (chi-square = 120, p = 6.3e-28; Fisher p = 2.1e-35; Cohen h = 3.14; PM4Py fitness 1.0, precision 0.98 on the discovered model). We contribute an operational distinction: hard controls for failures, soft SLAs for temporal variation.

## Keywords

process mining; conformance checking; procure-to-pay; ERP configuration; Odoo; control redesign; temporal variation; alignments; statistical evaluation

## Contributions

1. **Normative model with executable semantics.** A six-step P2P model with five formalised rules (R1–R5) expressed as ordering, attribute, and rework predicates, each traced to scenarios and log signatures (see `docs/01-normative-model.md` and `docs/10-appendix.md`, Section A).
2. **Controlled stochastic dataset with frozen checker.** A reproducible lab log (120 Round-1 cases / 740 events, seed 42; 60 Round-2 cases / 400 events, seed 7) that separates temporal delays (S2, S5) from behavioural control failures (S3, S4, S6) via lognormal waits under a business-hours clock, evaluated by an identical frozen rule-based checker (`src/check_conformance.py`) and a PM4Py discovery-and-alignment pipeline (`src/mining_pm4py.py`).
3. **Triangulated quantitative evidence.** Detection of 60/60 targeted deviations with zero false positives in the compliant baseline and elimination to 0/60 after redesign, quantified by chi-square (120, p = 6.3e-28), Fisher exact (p = 2.1e-35), Wilson 95% CIs ([0.940, 1.000] vs [0.000, 0.060]), Cohen h (3.14), PM4Py token-based fitness (1.0) and precision (0.98), and a Welch throughput test (S1 vs S2, t = −6.94, p = 3.3e-08), with a measured control–efficiency trade-off (+1 day re-approval overhead in S6R).
4. **Transferable control-design distinction.** Evidence that hard ERP controls (receipt-based billing blocks, zero-tolerance 3-way matching, PO re-approval gates) eliminate compliance failures while soft SLA-based escalation preserves flexibility for temporal variation, with external plausibility and falsifiers examined against the BPIC 2019 P2P log (`docs/07-real-dataset.md`).
